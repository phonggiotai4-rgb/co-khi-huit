Posted: July 8 — Nelly Williams, New York

Life is so busy these days that it often feels like we don't have enough time to cook a real meal. We eat too many snacks, and we don't eat enough fruit and vegetables. Can you tell me what people eat in your country and how healthy it is?

Nikos Samara, Athens

In Greece, we use a lot of olives, garlic, lemon, and herbs in our food, all healthy ingredients. But the important thing about Greece is when and how we eat. I know in other countries, people often eat a big meal in the evening, but that's too late! And I've also noticed that too many people eat very quickly, or even eat a sandwich while they're walking down the street! In Greece, everyone comes together in the middle of the day, and we have our main meal. We eat slowly and talk to each other!

Erika Tanaka, Tokyo

In Japan, we eat a lot of fish. Tuna and salmon are very popular, and they're very healthy. We eat fish for breakfast, lunch, and dinner! We also eat a lot of rice and vegetables. The most important thing about our diet is portion size. We don't have large meals. Instead, we have small portions of many different dishes, so we get lots of different vitamins and minerals. This is one reason why Japanese people live such a long time!

Vijay Kumar, Delhi

In India, we use lots of spices, and we use herbs in many of our dishes. Indian food is very tasty and healthy! Many people in India are vegetarian, so they don't eat any meat or fish. Happy People

1 — We all want to be healthy. 9.1 too, too much/many, (not) enough

Too, too much, and too many have a negative meaning. We use them to say that there is more than we want or need. Too is used with adjectives, too much is used with uncountable nouns, and too many is used with countable nouns:

This sandwich is too big. I can't eat it all. · There's too much salt in the soup. It's awful! · There are too many people here. I can't sit down.

We use enough to mean the right amount for what we want or need. Enough comes before nouns, but after adjectives:

We have enough money for the trip. · Do we have enough cups for everyone? · It's warm enough to sit outside.

We use not enough to say that the quality or amount is less than we want or need:

There aren't enough chairs at the table. · The music isn't loud enough. I can't hear it.   ▶ 9.1

9.2 First conditional

We use the first conditional to talk about possible situations in the future. It has an if-clause and a main clause:

If-clause (action)Main clause (result)
+If I pass my exams,I'll go to college.
–If he doesn't arrive soon,we won't catch the train.

We use the simple present in the if-clause. We usually use will + base form in the main clause:

If you listen to me, you'll know what to do. · If she doesn't prepare for the interview, she won't get the job.

The if-clause can come first or second:

If I come in first in the race, I'll win a prize. · I'll win a prize if I come in first in the race.   ▶ 9.2

9.3 Adverbs of manner

We use adverbs of manner to say how someone does something or how something happens: She was talking loudly. · Her heart's beating slowly.

Most are formed by adding -ly to an adjective:

AdjectiveAdverb
He's a quick learner.He learns very quickly.
Please be careful with the box.Put the box down carefully.

If an adjective ends in -le, we remove the -e and add -y: gentle → gently

If an adjective ends in -y, we remove the -y and add -ily: easy → easily

Some adverbs of manner are irregular: good → well, hard → hard, fast → fast   ▶ 9.3

The Iceman

When we think of healthy lifestyles, we don't usually imagine swimming under ice or running a marathon barefoot in the snow. But that is exactly what Wim Hof, the Dutch extreme athlete, is known for. Called "The Iceman," he has become famous for his ability to withstand freezing temperatures for long periods.

An unhappy event in his life, the sadness he felt after the death of his first wife, led him to using this method. However, 30 years later, he's still at it. His successes include the Guinness World Record for swimming the longest distance under ice in 2000, and the fastest half marathon barefoot on ice and snow in 2007. He has also climbed several mountains wearing nothing but shorts and shoes, including Mount Everest in Nepal and Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania.

However, Wim Hof believes that his method can help anyone live a healthier and happier life. People following it say they sleep better, have less stress, and feel energized and more focused. According to Wim Hof, you might feel lightheaded, but practicing these exercises reduces stress and prepares you for cold temperatures. So next, you're ready for "cold therapy." But is the Wim Hof method safe? For Wim Hof, it seems to be so far. Doctors have found that he uses his mind to control his body.
